title: Restore — shut the machine down, and your agents carry on
media: /media/superterm-restore-reboot

Shut a machine down, or lose tmux altogether, and superterm puts it back:
the same sessions, windows, and splits, each pane in its own directory, and
every coding agent resumed in the conversation it was having, by its id.
There is nothing to run. When superterm starts and finds no tmux server, or
sees the one it was watching disappear, it restores from its last snapshot.

In the clip, superterm is running in a Slicer VM with two opencode agents
that have just finished their tasks. The VM is shut down from the host and
relaunched. No commands are typed after that: the sessions come back, and
both agents are back in their conversations, on the same changes.

- Snapshots are taken when superterm is stopped, so a reboot or shutdown is
  covered, as well as when the layout changes and every few minutes.
- Claude Code, Codex, opencode, and Copilot resume by their exact
  conversation id. `superterm agent-setup install` adds the hooks that report
  it. An agent without an id gets its pane back with a note of how to resume.
- Plain shells come back in their directory with their recent output.
  Background jobs are named in that note, and are not restarted.
- A session you closed on purpose stays closed.
- `superterm restore --list` shows the snapshots; `superterm up
  --auto-restore=false` turns it off.
